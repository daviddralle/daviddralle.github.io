# Bill’s field haikus — research and editorial record

Rewritten September 17, 2026. The 100 poems are original compositions inspired by 13 papers coauthored by William E. Dietrich. Four papers include David Dralle, Jesse Hahm, and Daniella Rempe. Source keys live with each poem in `bill-haikus.js`; citations stay out of the overhead speech bubble.

The reading pass used publisher abstracts, accessible article text (including the lithology paper’s conclusions and the isotope paper’s interpretation), and Eel River CZO publication records. Publisher/observatory links below record the source used; DOI links identify the papers. Older geomorphology papers concern other California/Oregon landscapes as well as concepts relevant here, and are not labelled Rivendell observations.

## Editorial approach

Use a specific image, a physical process, and a change in perspective. Favor field objects and observations over generic nature sentiments. Keep the three-line, approximately 5–7–5 form and readable line lengths. Preserve the distinctions between soil moisture, unsaturated rock moisture, and groundwater; between indirect storage and discharge-sensitive storage; and between a conditional model and a universal rule. Poems are scenes and metaphors, not reports of particular measured events.

## Reading list and poem mapping

### Rempe, D. M., and Dietrich, W. E. (2018)

**[Direct observations of rock moisture, a hidden component of the hydrologic cycle](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1800141115)**

Repeated borehole neutron measurements track winter recharge and summer withdrawal of water held in unsaturated weathered bedrock. Trees use this store after shallow soils dry.

Poems 1–10 · `rock-moisture` · source key `rempe2018`. [Reading source](https://czo-archive.criticalzone.org/eel/publications/pub/rempe-dietrich-2018-direct-observations-of-rock-moisture-a-hidden-component/).

### Dralle, D. N., Hahm, W. J., Rempe, D. M., Karst, N. J., Thompson, S. E., and Dietrich, W. E. (2018)

**[Quantification of the seasonal hillslope water storage that does not drive streamflow](https://doi.org/10.1002/hyp.11627)**

Discharge responds to only part of changing seasonal storage. The indirect component can support transpiration, and equal discharge need not imply equal total storage.

Poems 11–20 · `indirect-storage` · source key `dralle2018`. [Reading source](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/hyp.11627).

### Hahm, W. J., Rempe, D. M., Dralle, D. N., Dawson, T. E., Lovill, S. M., Bryk, A. B., Bish, D. L., Schieber, J., and Dietrich, W. E. (2019)

**[Lithologically Controlled Subsurface Critical Zone Thickness and Water Storage Capacity Determine Regional Plant Community Composition](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018WR023760)**

Under similar climate, deeply weathered Coastal Belt rock supports evergreen forest and sustained summer flow; a thin weathered zone in Central Belt mélange supports oak savanna and seasonal streams.

Poems 21–30 · `bedrock-and-forest` · source key `hahm2019lithology`. [Reading source](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2018WR023760).

### Hahm, W. J., Dralle, D. N., Rempe, D. M., Bryk, A. B., Thompson, S. E., Dawson, T. E., and Dietrich, W. E. (2019)

**[Low Subsurface Water Storage Capacity Relative to Annual Rainfall Decouples Mediterranean Plant Productivity and Water Use From Rainfall Variability](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019GL083294)**

Where both wetter and drier winters refill a limited subsurface store, summer plant water availability varies less than annual rainfall. The refill condition matters; this is not immunity to all drought.

Poems 31–38 · `storage-capacity` · source key `hahm2019capacity`. [Reading source](https://czo-archive.criticalzone.org/eel/publications/pub/hahm-et-al-2019-low-subsurface-water-storage-capacity-relative-to-annual-ra/).

### Hahm, W. J., Rempe, D. M., Dralle, D. N., Dawson, T. E., and Dietrich, W. E. (2020)

**[Oak Transpiration Drawn From the Weathered Bedrock Vadose Zone in the Summer Dry Season](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020WR027419)**

Isotopes and declining rock moisture support summer oak use of unsaturated weathered rock, rather than nearby groundwater in poorly oxygenated, low-permeability fresh rock.

Poems 39–46 · `oak-water-sources` · source key `hahm2020oak`. [Reading source](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2020WR027419).

### Oshun, J., Dietrich, W. E., Dawson, T. E., and Fung, I. (2016)

**[Dynamic, structured heterogeneity of water isotopes inside hillslopes](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015WR017485)**

Storm isotope variability is damped in groundwater; mobile and more tightly held water can differ even within one hillslope. Sampling method and material matter.

Poems 47–54 · `water-isotopes` · source key `oshun2016`. [Reading source](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/2015WR017485).

### Rempe, D. M., and Dietrich, W. E. (2014)

**[A bottom-up control on fresh-bedrock topography under landscapes](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1404763111)**

A testable model links channel incision, drainage of fresh rock, and the advance of weathering. It predicts a weathered zone that thickens toward the ridge.

Poems 55–62 · `weathering-front` · source key `rempe2014`. [Reading source](https://czo-archive.criticalzone.org/eel/publications/pub/rempe-dietrich-2014-a-bottom-up-control-on-fresh-bedrock-topography-under-l/).

### Salve, R., Rempe, D. M., and Dietrich, W. E. (2012)

**[Rain, rock moisture dynamics, and the rapid response of perched groundwater in weathered, fractured argillite underlying a steep hillslope](https://doi.org/10.1029/2012WR012583)**

Some wells respond before a deep wetting front is observed, consistent with preferential fracture flow. Neighboring wells can respond very differently to one storm.

Poems 63–68 · `fracture-recharge` · source key `salve2012`. [Reading source](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1029/2012WR012583).

### Montgomery, D. R., and Dietrich, W. E. (1988)

**[Where do channels begin?](https://doi.org/10.1038/336232a0)**

Mapped channel heads show an inverse relation between source area and slope, with landslide initiation important in steep humid terrain. An unchannelled valley can extend above the channel head.

Poems 69–76 · `channel-heads` · source key `montgomery1988`. [Reading source](https://www.nature.com/articles/336232a0).

### Montgomery, D. R., and Dietrich, W. E. (1994)

**[A physically based model for the topographic control on shallow landsliding](https://doi.org/10.1029/93WR02979)**

Coupling topographically concentrated subsurface flow to slope stability explains why steep convergent hollows are prone to shallow landslides.

Poems 77–84 · `shallow-landslides` · source key `montgomery1994`. [Reading source](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/93WR02979).

### Heimsath, A. M., Dietrich, W. E., Nishiizumi, K., and Finkel, R. C. (1997)

**[The soil production function and landscape equilibrium](https://doi.org/10.1038/41056)**

Northern California measurements using curvature and cosmogenic nuclides indicate declining soil production with increasing soil thickness. A steady form can coexist with continual material replacement.

Poems 85–90 · `soil-production` · source key `heimsath1997`. [Reading source](https://www.nature.com/articles/41056).

### Roering, J. J., Kirchner, J. W., and Dietrich, W. E. (1999)

**[Evidence for nonlinear, diffusive sediment transport on hillslopes and implications for landscape morphology](https://doi.org/10.1029/1998WR900090)**

A nonlinear transport law links rounded hilltops and nearly planar steep flanks. Near a critical gradient, small slope changes can accompany large changes in soil flux.

Poems 91–95 · `hillslope-transport` · source key `roering1999`. [Reading source](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1029/1998WR900090).

### Sklar, L. S., and Dietrich, W. E. (2004)

**[A mechanistic model for river incision into bedrock by saltating bed load](https://doi.org/10.1029/2003WR002496)**

Moving sediment abrades exposed bedrock, but abundant sediment can protect it by covering the bed. Erosion is greatest at intermediate relative sediment supply.

Poems 96–100 · `bedrock-incision` · source key `sklar2004`. [Reading source](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2003WR002496).

## Scope and attribution checks

- The four Dralle collaborations are the 2018 indirect-storage paper, both 2019 Hahm papers, and the 2020 oak-water-source paper.
- Rock moisture is water within unsaturated weathered bedrock, not water coating loose stones and not a synonym for groundwater.
- Small storage limits sensitivity to rainfall variation only when winter rainfall replenishes it. Poems 33–38 keep replenishment explicit.
- The oak water-source poems describe the study’s unsaturated source; the nearby saturated zone is not assumed to be plant-accessible.
- A well’s quick storm response does not establish the travel time or age of every water molecule.
- Soil replacement, nonlinear transport, and tools-versus-cover are drawn from older studies; the poems do not imply that the atlas measured those rates.
- No quotations or invented anecdotes are attributed to Bill or his coauthors.
