Source discrepancies and interpretation
  • WS1 burning: the current infrastructure table gives 1967, while the detailed HF004 history and HS004 metadata give October 1966. This summary uses the detailed account and preserves the discrepancy.
  • Drainage area: surveyed gauge areas and 2018 GIS areas represent different delineations. Do not silently substitute polygon area when converting archived discharge to runoff depth.
  • Older boundaries: the HF014 legacy catalog is marked “Restricted: QA/QC issues.” This map uses the separately published 2018 public hydrology layer, not a recovered HF014 archive.
  • Elevation: WS3 and Mack gauge elevations differ slightly between the infrastructure table and the detailed history. This page uses the detailed gauge entries.
  • Terrain coverage: all eight small-catchment clips have 100% valid terrain coverage; the Mack clip has 99.997% coverage. NoData is retained. The hillshade is resampled for display and is not a new elevation measurement.
  • Hydrologic inference: surface boundaries do not independently establish groundwater contributing areas. Treatment histories alone do not establish a causal streamflow effect.

Primary documentation

  3. GI010 lidar catalog and terrain metadata — native 2008 terrain, processing and reference systems.
  4. HJ Andrews Hydrology (2018) — layers 5 (catchments), 8 (hydrologic boundary), and 1 (streams).

Two starting papers

Jones & Post (2004). Seasonal and successional streamflow response to forest cutting and regrowth. Useful framing for separating seasonal responses from stand-age changes.

Perry & Jones (2017). Summer streamflow deficits from regenerating Douglas-fir forest. A basis for later low-flow comparisons; published effects are not recalculated on this page.

For the next data stage

No discharge, climate, chemistry, or temperature time series have been downloaded. This package establishes catchment IDs, treatment histories, spatial data, and provenance first.

Start with HF004 discharge and its daily entity, preserving quality flags, rating-curve changes, missing periods and Pacific Standard Time conventions. Check the documented low-flow leakage and gauge interruptions before comparing basins.

Before extending comparisons beyond 2020, overlay the fire-severity layers and define the analysis periods explicitly. The HJA imagery page provides Holiday Farm and Lookout fire products.
