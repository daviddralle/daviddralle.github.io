Model and decision methods

Decision method

To minimize expected annual spending, buy when the annual premium is less than the expected annual insurance payout. Expected uninsured cost is E[damage]; expected insured cost is premium + E[damage − payout]. Payout accounts for the deductible, coverage exclusions and limit.

A household may rationally pay more than expected payout to avoid an unaffordable loss. A decision analysis should therefore also compare the probability of out-of-pocket costs exceeding the owner’s chosen budget, and test the result across plausible flood-frequency, hydraulic and repair-cost models.

The current annual insurance weights and repair amounts are editable placeholders. Measured flow statistics have not yet been converted to calibrated property-loss probabilities, so the main conclusion describes event-level protection. It is not a calibrated break-even premium or actuarial buy/no-buy verdict. The policy limit, deductible and covered share also need confirmation.

Method references: USACE flood damage analysis · MIT: risk aversion and insurance.

Methods and limitations

The calculation follows the general stage → depth → damage → payout approach described by USACE HEC-FDA. The editable repair amounts are analyst-created placeholders; they are not USACE damage curves or local contractor estimates. NFIP coverage guidance informs which terms need verification; this is not a determination of coverage.

Water elevations come from the atlas’s county depth + 2013 terrain reconstruction at integer gauge stages. Certificate building elevations are fixed. The 2019 waterline check remains unresolved. No new HEC-RAS simulation, flood-frequency fit, or El Niño adjustment is used here. A 45-ft scenario approximates the gauge level of 2019; it does not reconstruct the event.

Model: damage below the living floor is zero. Living-area loss = editable depth-cost curve, zero at/below the living floor and the first-wetting cost immediately above it; costs are held at the 10-ft value beyond that depth. Eligible loss = living-area loss × its eligible share. Payout = min(limit, max(0, eligible loss − deductible)). Retained loss = total modeled loss − payout. Annual results are probability-weighted sums over the five scenarios; other years receive zero loss. No depreciation, inflation, premium increases, waiting periods, structural failure/erosion losses beyond the allowances, or claims disputes are modeled.
