Model and methods

Annual-maximum distributions

The default is a generalized extreme-value distribution fitted by L-moments to USGS annual peak discharge at Hacienda Bridge (11467000). It is an established extreme-value method, not a claim that one family is uniquely best. A Gumbel fit (the zero-shape GEV limit), also using L-moments, provides a simpler two-parameter comparison. A preliminary Log-Pearson III fit using unadjusted log moments produced upper bounds below observed floods in several groups, so it is excluded from decisions; diagnostics are in the downloadable fit data. This is not a Bulletin 17C analysis: there is no EMA, regional skew weighting, historical censoring, or low-outlier adjustment.

1984–2025 is the default because Lake Sonoma regulation began in October 1983. The full record combines reservoir regimes. All available peaks in the selected record are used; regulated flows are not naturalized. Separate El Niño and other-winter fits use NOAA ERSSTv6 RONI's qualified warm episode classification for DJF of the ending water year. Pre-1950 years have no classification and are excluded from seasonal subsets. El Niño is a conditional historical comparison, not a forecast or a causal effect.

The plot band and interval use 2,000 nonparametric year-resampling bootstrap fits (seed 24092026). These are pointwise 95% sampling intervals conditional on the chosen family and fixed house threshold; they omit hydraulic, rating, future-climate, and reservoir-operation uncertainty. El Niño samples are particularly small. Plot dots use rank/(n+1), while observed counts use k/n.

Fixed surveyed living floor; provisional flow transfer

The county HEC-RAS depth rasters are reconstructed to water elevation using the atlas terrain. The county's original model terrain was from 2015; the reconstruction uses 2013 terrain. Linear interpolation between stage scenarios finds first contact with the fixed 45.6-ft NAVD88 living floor. It does not move the floor or use a road-level assumption.

Hacienda discharge and Johnsons Beach stage are different gauges. The working discharge threshold is the mean of linearly interpolated, simultaneous Hacienda flows when Johnsons Beach stage crossed the modeled house threshold on the rising and falling limbs of the February 2019 flood. This single-event relationship is a transfer scenario, not an official rating curve or a validated annual-peak mapping. Hydrograph timing, backwater and channel changes can alter it; a multi-event validation and original county flow profiles remain needed.

The model predicts living-floor inundation in 2019; the owner's earlier recollection of a dry living floor has not been resolved by a surveyed waterline. Confirmation of high street water does not independently validate interior depth. The bootstrap interval does not cover this discrepancy. The CNRFC historical table's estimated 2019 discharge differs from the USGS 72,000-cfs peak; this analysis consistently uses USGS discharge.

Minimum expected annual cost

Each year has either zero damage or one house-flood loss of the entered amount. Damage is zero at or below the living floor, including water beneath the house. The entered damage amount is a fixed, fully eligible building-loss scenario conditional on a house flood. It is not a fitted depth–damage curve or an estimate of this building's actual repairs.

For flood probability p, damage D, deductible d, limit L, and annual premium P:

Payout B = min(L, max(0, D − d))
Without insurance = p × D
With insurance = P + p × (D − B)
Break-even premium = p × B

Insurance minimizes expected dollars when P < pB. This objective excludes risk aversion, liquidity, financing, taxes, inflation, exclusions, contents, repeated floods in one year, and discounting. If the damage input is instead the mean of a variable loss distribution, payout at that mean need not equal mean payout because deductible and limit are nonlinear. Policy terms and premium remain unverified; $10,000 is provisionally treated as the deductible, $250,000 as the limit, and $100,000 is an editable damage scenario.

The 10-year probability assumes independent years and stationary risk. With El Niño off, it is 1 − (1 − p)10. With El Niño on, only the first year uses the conditional El Niño probability; the following nine use the all-year fit for the selected period. It does not assume ten consecutive El Niño winters.

Inputs save locally under a new binary-model schema. The previous five-stage placeholder probability weights and depth–damage assumptions are not used.
